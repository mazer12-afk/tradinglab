Two weeks, 80 accounts: what the profit vault banked

15 Oct 23:47 → 29 Oct 23:47 UTC. 20 strategies, each run four times: once with no vault (the control) and once each with a vault that banks all profit above $100,000 once equity reaches +1 %, +2 % or +4 %. Every account started at $100,000. Figures are as of the close-out at 23:48 UTC, at live prices after fees, spread and funding.

Verdict

The vault banked real money. No strategy proved real skill.

The market fell, two-thirds of it on the last day. Only 4 of the 20 strategies made money: three daily ones that were about half net short, and LightGBM 4h, which made most of its +7.7 % in a few hours with an unusually large book. The 20 strategies move together like about three independent bets, and the best of such a field reaching +7.7 % is roughly what luck alone would produce.

The vault rule behaved exactly as specified: 33 sweeps, each triggered at its threshold, closed the book at live prices and moved the profit out of reach. Net of everything, banking added $11,753 across the 14 twins that swept, almost all of it in the daily deep-learning books, which kept giving back gains after each high. Whether that pattern holds outside this fortnight is untested.

What to do with this

  • Do not promote anything to real money on these two weeks. In the daily book, average net exposure alone explains 70 % of the spread in returns: the three winners (LSTM, DeePM-lite, Breakout) were about 50 % net short while the 30-coin basket fell 14 %. In the 5-minute book, costs decided more than direction.
  • Keep LightGBM 4h running, but fix its sizing first. All of its profit came in the 22 % of the time its book was above 0.9× equity. Its selective policy freezes each coin's size at entry, so the book drifted up to 1.37×, far above the roughly 0.4× its volatility target sets on a fresh entry. 70 % of its gain came on the last day. Its vault comparisons rest on the same sizing quirk, so they say little about which threshold is best.
  • Fix the drawdown guard before any real money. It halted the deep market-neutral strategy at −40 % on 22 Oct, re-armed itself 100 minutes later at full size, and the account went on to −67 %. A halt should hold until someone reviews it.
  • Retire the deep market-neutral strategy. It re-traded about 10 of its 12 coins on almost every 5-minute bar and paid $70,544 in fees and spread to lose $67,393. In a replay, no no-trade band or smoothing setting showed a meaningful edge underneath.
  • Run the next round for 2–3 months and across an up-move, with the vault twins kept as they are. Two weeks of one falling market, decided largely on its last day, cannot separate a good model from a short position.
1 · Market

A late slide: 29 of 30 coins fell

The equal-weight basket of the 12 intraday coins fell 9.8 % (worst drawdown −12.4 %); every one of them fell, from Bitcoin Cash −1.5 % to Polkadot −19.8 %. The daily book's 30-coin basket fell 14.4 %, with TRX the only coin up (+5.7 %). Bitcoin fell 4.6 %. Much of it came at the very end: the 12-coin basket was down 3.5 % after 13 days, then lost another 6.5 % on 29 Oct, 5.0 % of that in the hour to 21:00 UTC (the 30-coin basket: −7.3 %, then −7.7 %, with −5.7 % in that hour).

2 · Strategies

Who made money, and how

Each strategy's no-vault control. "Net exp." is the average net position as a share of equity (negative = net short). Beta and alpha come from a regression of hourly returns on the equal-weight basket of the book's coins (12 for the 5-minute book, 30 for the daily book); an alpha t-statistic below about 2 is indistinguishable from zero. Costs are fees, spread and net funding.

14-day return without a vault
In the daily book, direction explains most of it

Every daily strategy that was net long lost money, and the three daily winners were the most net-short (about −0.5). Across the 14 daily strategies the correlation between return and average net exposure is −0.83 (−0.59 by rank). The 5-minute book is different: its three biggest losers lost to fees and spread, and LightGBM 4h, net short on average, made $4,662 of its $7,687 in the 8 % of the time it was net long. None of the 20 strategies has an alpha t-statistic above 0.8.

Your two suggested models

DeePM-lite (daily) made +3.2 % and is one of the four winners, again while about 55 % net short. The Momentum Transformer (daily) was the worst daily strategy at −9.2 %, with the highest turnover in the daily book. The main production blend, chosen by the earlier backtests, lost 7.3 %, more than the 30-coin long basket held at similar exposure (−5.1 %).

3 · The vault

What banking the profit did

A vault twin trades exactly like its control until its marked equity reaches $101k, $102k or $104k. At that moment it closes every position at live prices, moves everything above $100,000 into the vault and starts again from $100,000. Money in the vault is never traded. 14 of the 60 twins reached their threshold at least once; the other 46 never did and finished level with their control (the three Momentum Transformer twins sit $4.67 lower because of a quote-timing quirk at the very first trade).

Why the daily deep-learning twins came out ahead

The daily book decides once a day, around 03:00 UTC, so a twin that banked during the day stayed flat until the next decision, for up to 17 hours. Across the 10 distinct sweep times, the net-short controls mostly gave back part of their gains while the twins sat flat. The 29 Oct sweep at 21:00 landed exactly on the low of a 5.7 % one-hour drop in the 30-coin basket; the controls then lost $1.3k–1.8k as their short positions bounced. That one sweep is the whole gain of both 4 % twins.

Why the LightGBM 4h twins fell behind

The control's selective policy freezes each coin's size at entry, so by 18 Oct its book had grown to 1.16× equity. After the 18 Oct sweeps, the 1 % and 2 % twins re-entered all 12 positions at once, sized by the volatility target at about 0.4×. When those positions rallied, the twins gained far less: about $2,000 and $1,400 behind the control in that episode alone. The 4 % twin's $444 shortfall comes from less capital at work, gains missed while flat, and closing costs.

What banking did here, and what it costs in general

Across the 14 twins that swept, banking added $11,753 net: +$16,001 for the six daily deep-learning twins, −$3,987 for the three LightGBM 4h twins and −$261 for the rest. The daily gain is not an accident of ordering: shuffling the controls' hourly returns removes it (the observed result sits in the top 0.5–6 % of shuffles), and for the 1 % and 2 % twins it stays significant even without the last day. It came from a pattern of this fortnight: these net-short books gave back gains after each equity high, and the vault caught them at the high. Whether that pattern holds in other markets is untested.

The only cost built into the rule is the round trip at each sweep, $70–260 per daily sweep here. Everything else depends on what the strategy does next. What it buys is certainty: $60,462 sits in the 14 vaults, out of reach whatever happens next.

Every one of the 33 sweeps was checked: each fired on the first mark at or above its threshold, closed every position, moved exactly the realised cash above $100,000, and restarted flat at $100,000.00. No threshold crossing went unbanked, and nothing ever left a vault.

4 · Losses

Where the money went

Deep market-neutral: −67.4 %, almost all of it costs

38,989 fills in 14 days, a turnover of 1,024 times the starting capital. Fees and spread came to $70,544; before them the book was +$3,151.

The cause sits in how the strategy was built. The deep model's outputs were almost identical for all 12 coins (they differed by about 0.5 % of their level). Subtracting the average left only that noise, and volatility targeting scaled the noise up to a book worth 1.5× equity on average. Each coin's position flipped sign roughly every hour, and about a quarter of the book was re-traded every 5 minutes. A replay with wider no-trade bands or smoothed targets found no setting with a meaningful edge: without any costs at all, the replayed policy made +5.3 % at constant capital (t ≈ 1.3), about 0.3 bp per dollar traded against roughly 7 bp of real cost. The drawdown guard halted it twice, at −40 % and later lower, and re-armed itself both times after 100 minutes.

Deep LSTM-attention: a long basket in disguise

Its outputs were positive and nearly equal across coins, and the position sizing ignores a common scale factor, so it held the same vol-targeted long basket as the benchmark: weights within 5 % of each other, equity never more than $78 apart, −4.1 % against −4.2 %. In this period the model added nothing.

Momentum 4h rule and LightGBM 1h: costs

The 4h momentum rule lost 6.6 %: $6,267 of fees and spread on 860 fills, with trading itself slightly negative before them (−$319). LightGBM 1h made $1,156 before fees and spread and paid $2,298 in them, ending −1.1 %.

Main blend: market exposure

The production blend lost 7.3 % while paying only $278 in costs. It held about 46 % net long through the slide and finished 2.2 points behind the long basket held at similar exposure. The "Hold BTC" benchmark is a 35 % BTC position, so its −1.9 % compares with −4.6 % for Bitcoin itself.

5 · Skill or luck

Two weeks cannot tell them apart

Each strategy's two-week result has a noise band: the spread its own hourly returns would give if it had no edge at all. For LightGBM 4h that band is ±6.7 %, so its +7.7 % (7.4 % as a log return) is 1.1 standard deviations, a result a strategy with no edge reaches about 13 % of the time.

But 20 strategies were in the race, and they move together: their hourly returns have an average correlation of 0.35 and behave like about three independent bets. Resampling all 20 together in one-day blocks, with each one's average return removed, the best of 20 edge-free strategies typically shows +6.5 % to +7 % and beats +7.4 % in about 40 % of draws. So +7.7 % is roughly what the best of this field would show by luck. The ranking also hangs on the last day: LightGBM 4h made about $5,400 of its $7,687 on 29 Oct, and Breakout was slightly down before it.

Resampling each strategy's own hourly returns in one-day blocks gives the same picture: the 90 % ranges of all four winners include zero (LightGBM 4h −2.4 % to +19.6 %, LSTM −1.6 % to +10.4 %, DeePM-lite −2.2 % to +9.6 %, Breakout −3.7 % to +9.7 %).

Close-out incident, fixed and excluded

At 23:48 UTC on 29 Oct the close-out flattened every account at live prices as designed. Saving the final status then failed on a timestamp format, so the system retried every few minutes, and each retry first ran a normal 5-minute trading cycle. Over the next hour the 24 intraday accounts traded again after the end: 4,952 fills costing $9,550 in fees and $3,435 in spread. I stopped the retries at 01:04 UTC, restored the records to the first close-out, and fixed the code (the close-out no longer trades, and timestamps parse at any precision). Those post-end trades are not part of these results; the discarded state stays in the branch history.

Fills are simulated at live quotes: the spread, plus an impact charge (1 bp intraday, 5 bp daily), plus a 5 bp fee, executed instantly. Real fills on a busy 5-minute book would cost somewhat more. Funding uses estimated rates where the exchange rate was unavailable (all of the daily book) and is small: no account paid or received more than $212.

One small engine flaw showed up in the daily book's first cycle: a 10-second quote cache expired while accounts were being filled, so the three Momentum Transformer twins sold three coins at a slightly lower bid than their control. That is the only reason those twins differ from their control (by $4.67). One quote snapshot per cycle, as the 5-minute engine already uses, removes it.
