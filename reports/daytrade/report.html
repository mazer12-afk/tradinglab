Day-trading research · 12 crypto perpetuals on Binance · 1-minute bars · developed Aug 2023 – Oct 2025 · tested 29 Oct 2025 – 29 Oct 2026

Day trading: one edge found, gone in the test year

Every idea was developed on data up to 29 Oct 2025 and judged after costs. The one rule that survived was written down and committed to the repository before the following year was simulated once. Simulated fills use 1-minute bars: limit orders count only when the price trades through them, stops are assumed to fill before targets, and fees are Binance's 2 bp for limit orders and 5 bp plus spread for market orders.

Verdict

No day-trading strategy made money in the test year

The best candidate, buying sharp crashes with limit orders and selling four hours later, lost 1.9 % in the test year. Its edge per trade before fees fell from +80 bp in 2023–24 to +56 bp in 2024–25 and to +4 bp in the test year, below the 7 bp of fees each trade pays (spread and impact are already deducted from the edge).

Every other idea tested, including the lab's 5-minute AI models, had too little edge to pay for its trading even in development. The only effect that looked large was the rebound after crash-like moves, and it faded year by year.

What to do with this

  • Do not day trade real money with any of these rules. None kept an edge into the following year.
  • Costs are the hurdle. At retail fees a trade costs 7–16 bp, more than any repeatable effect found in minute and 5-minute bars.
  • If you want to continue, change the inputs, not the parameters. Order-book and trade-by-trade data, or fee tiers near zero (market-maker rebates), would move the hurdle. Re-tuning these rules on more history would only fit the past better.

2 · Fading extreme moves

Fading both directions worked for a year, then failed

Take the other side of a 5-minute move beyond k standard deviations with a limit order, hold up to 4 hours. On the design period all 81 variants of the extended grid made money (best: Sharpe 2.2, +72 % a year). On the second half of development all 81 lost (the same variant: Sharpe −0.75, −30 %). Splitting the trades by direction showed why.

Crashes bounced and spikes kept going, in both halves. That turned the idea into a long-only rule. Because it came from looking at both halves, neither could confirm it any more; only the untouched test year could.

3 · Buy the crash

The frozen rule and its one test

After a 5-minute close more than 8 standard deviations below the previous one, post a limit buy 1 standard deviation lower, valid 10 minutes; sell at market 4 hours after the fill. No stop, no target; 20 % of equity per trade, open positions capped at 1× equity. Chosen as the best of 64 long-only variants on the design period, then committed before the test.

4 · Method

How the simulator fills a trade

  • Decisions use bars up to the close of the decision minute; orders fill from the next minute on.
  • Market orders fill at the next minute's open plus the coin's typical live half-spread and 1 bp of impact, paying 5 bp. Limit orders fill at their price only if a later minute trades at least 1 bp through it, paying 2 bp; unfilled, they expire.
  • A stop fills before a take-profit when both are reached in the same minute, at the stop or the worse open. After a limit entry, exits are checked from the next minute.
  • Funding is paid or received at every settlement held through. One trade per coin at a time; total open positions capped.
  • Seven tests in tests/test_daytrade.py check these rules on hand-built bars, including that nothing before or at the decision minute can change a fill.
  • Protocol: ideas and parameters on Aug 2023 – Aug 2024; checked on Sep 2024 – Oct 2025; one frozen rule run once on 29 Oct 2025 – 29 Oct 2026.
