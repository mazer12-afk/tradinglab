One method passed every test: funding carry

Every trading rule in this lab now has to pass the same nine checks, on data it had never seen, with the rule frozen before the test. Eight rules went through. Five failed. The three that passed are versions of one method, funding carry, and it also held up on a second exchange and on coins that were later delisted. It is low-risk and it only earns when the market is bullish.

Verdict

Proven on past data; the live check finishes on 6 Dec

Funding carry buys a coin on the spot market and shorts the same amount on its perpetual future. The two price moves cancel out. What is left is the funding rate, a fee that holders of the future pay to the short side when the market is leaning long.

The simplest version holds that trade on Bitcoin and Ether all the time. From 2020 to Oct 2026 it made +7.4% a year on Binance with every year in profit and a worst drawdown of -1.2%, after fees on both legs. A second version trades any coin while its funding over the last 14 days is above 10 % a year. Run on every coin Binance ever listed on both markets (478 coins, 118 of them later delisted), it made +6.9% a year with a worst drawdown of -2.0%.

The return depends on the market. Both versions made 23 – 28 % in 2021 and about 8 % in 2024, and little in 2022, 2025 and 2026, when funding was low. Bitcoin's funding is running near 2.6 % a year now, so the method is earning little today.

What the failures show

Rules that bet on direction did not survive new data

  • A strong backtest is not evidence. R8 had a Sharpe of 2.0 over four years with every year positive, and lost 19 % on the next three. R1 made +29 % in the months it was built on and lost 64 % elsewhere.
  • Coin selection hides risk. Tests on today's most traded coins leave out the ones that died. Carry looked a little better on those hand-picked sets than on the complete universe.
  • Carry passes because it does not predict prices. It collects a fee that exists whenever the market leans long. That is also why it earns nothing when the market does not.
Running it

What it needs, and what can go wrong

  • An account where spot coins count as collateral for the short (unified or portfolio margin). Coins spike 46 – 66 % in a day about once in 1,000 coin-days. Without shared collateral the short could be liquidated and leave the spot leg unhedged.
  • Watching delisting notices. The worst single day in the test was a perpetual that rose 94 % on its last day while the spot price fell 45 %.
  • Sudden collapses. One coin fell 83 % in a day and its funding turned sharply negative; at a tenth of the book that cost 1.3 %. The per-coin cap is what limits this.
  • Exchange failure. No backtest covers an exchange freezing withdrawals. Money on one exchange carries that risk.
  • Patience. In markets like 2022 or today the rule mostly sits in cash.
Still open

The live paper test

Day 2 of at least 28 (through 2026-11-09). So far: 12-coin rule -0.02%, always BTC + ETH -0.09%, any-coin rule -0.13%. These are mostly the cost of opening positions. Live prices match Binance's archive exactly so far.

The test runs daily on live Binance data through the same code as the backtest. Its job is to show that the method trades live as simulated; four weeks cannot show profit. The verdict is due on or after 6 Dec 2026.
